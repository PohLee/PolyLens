# PR Crisis Recovery Playbook

Use this playbook when reputation damage, media scrutiny, or public backlash becomes decision-critical. Apply the same evidence standard to the organisation, critics, and the user's interpretation. Neutrality means evidence-weighted judgment, not equal weight for unsupported claims.

## When to Use

- A negative story, scandal, or backlash is spreading publicly
- Customers, employees, investors, or partners are losing trust
- You need a recovery narrative and response posture
- You need channel-specific messaging and trust repair actions

## Process

Complete gates 1-3 before selecting a response posture in any lens. Complete gates 4-5 before drafting publishable messaging. Carry the results into the existing lens/report format; separate sections are unnecessary when the required information is already explicit.

### 1. Evidence Ledger

Record the decision, timeline, affected channels, available statements, audience evidence, and research constraints. Classify each material claim:

- **SUPPORTED FACT** — identify the supporting source and its limits. A statement proves what was said, not necessarily that its contents are true.
- **USER-REPORTED** — attribute the account; use it as a conditional scenario, not independent verification.
- **INFERENCE / FORECAST** — state the evidence, alternative explanations, and uncertainty.
- **UNKNOWN / DISPUTED** — identify the missing evidence and how it affects the decision.

Separate observable conduct, applicable obligations, demonstrated impact, inferred intent, public interpretation, and causal claims. Check accusations of wrongdoing and claims that the organisation did nothing wrong by the same standard. Classify validity as supported, partly supported, contradicted, or unresolved; apply labels such as cultural insensitivity or misinformation only when justified.

Respect the user's research permissions. When web research is prohibited, use supplied/local material and preserve uncertainty; never imply independent verification. Ask only for missing information that changes the recommendation, or provide conditional branches. Treat absent evidence as unknown, not proof of innocence or guilt.

**Gate:** Every material claim has a status and source/basis. Legal conclusions identify the applicable jurisdiction, rule, and supporting authority, or remain unresolved. Intent, sentiment scale, and causality have not been inferred from an omission, virality, or a timeline alone.

### 2. Decision and Accountability Gate

Decompose the response into independent choices: acknowledgement, apology, clarification or denial; operational addition versus removal/replacement; timing; spokesperson; and distribution scope.

For an apology, specify the act or omission, evidence, responsibility being accepted, and what the wording does and does not concede. Distinguish apologising for a demonstrated failing from admitting illegality or discriminatory intent. A lawful action can still cause a supported customer harm; backlash alone does not establish a failing. An apology is neither universally required nor universally an admission of the accusation.

Compare full/limited accountability, factual clarification, evidence-backed denial, an interim acknowledgement, and monitored silence where amplification risk justifies it. Recommend each only when supported; unresolved material facts favour bounded acknowledgement or clarification over an unsupported admission or denial. Any urgent protective action for evidenced ongoing harm proceeds without waiting for reputational certainty.

**Gate:** Each proposed concession or denial names its evidence and scope. Missing evidence produces a conditional recommendation, not a default apology or defence.

### 3. Coupled-Signal and Stakeholder Test

Evaluate each action and the complete sequence. For every materially affected audience, record the intended message, plausible adverse interpretation, supporting evidence or forecast status, impact, mitigation, and residual uncertainty.

Test these mechanisms explicitly:

- **Admission signal:** Could acknowledgement, apology, wording, or executive visibility appear to validate a disputed accusation beyond the accepted responsibility?
- **Historical-neglect signal:** Could a rapid correction prompt "if it was easy, why not earlier?" Distinguish delivery speed from proof of past feasibility or intent. Use only documented explanations; keep timely improvements timely.
- **Displacement signal:** Does solving one concern remove another audience's language access, service, representation, or benefit? Compare addition, supplementary access, and replacement rather than treating them as equivalent.
- **Amplification and sequence:** Could a national statement enlarge a local issue, a screenshot strip away qualifications, or combined actions create a new accusation or a second backlash?

Map evidenced audience segments and within-group differences. Attribute boycott calls or comments to their sources; avoid assigning a shared view to an entire ethnic, religious, linguistic, or national community. Treat audience reactions as hypotheses until supported. Never engineer delay, obscure a necessary correction, or invent a historical explanation to avoid these questions.

**Gate:** The combined response has been tested for all four mechanisms, including secondary audiences. Each material risk has a mitigation, a safer alternative, or an explicit reason it remains acceptable.

### 4. Compare Options and Select Posture

Compare the proposed package with at least one credible alternative and a bounded holding/monitoring option where safe. Include benefits, evidence dependencies, admission and amplification risks, secondary impact, feasibility, reversibility, and uncertainty. Avoid numerical likelihoods or scores without a stated basis.

Choose the narrowest truthful, proportionate response supported by the evidence. Assign owners for missing facts and implementation. State the publication gate, next update trigger, and evidence that would change the recommendation. CEO, CMO, COO, or other lens priorities assess the same ledger; lens agreement is not additional factual evidence. Resolve uncertainty through evidence or conditional branches, not voting or automatic arbitration over what is true.

**Gate:** Explain why this combination is preferable to alternatives. Publishable messaging remains blocked wherever it depends on unresolved material facts; a factually bounded holding statement may proceed.

### 5. Build and Check the Message

- Use the approved posture and ledger; match the message's reach to the evidenced incident scale.
- State what is established, what is under review, and what responsibility is accepted, if any.
- Announce only approved, feasible actions; distinguish planned, underway, and verified complete.
- Keep the factual position consistent across channels and translations. Validate language with fluent reviewers; clearly label unreviewed drafts.
- Address legitimate criticism without ethnic blame, invented motives, fabricated supporters, or misleading moderation. Apply consistent safety/privacy rules.

**Gate:** Every factual assertion and promise is traceable to evidence or an authorised commitment. A reader seeing only the headline, apology, or action can still understand the accepted scope as far as practical.

### 6. Monitor Outcomes and Update Without Taking Sides

Track delivery, customer experience, recurring complaint themes, audience-specific reactions, and evidenced spread against a stated baseline where available. Include owners, review times, and escalation triggers. Lower comment volume alone does not prove restored trust; virality does not establish representative community sentiment.

When challenged, record what changed: new evidence, a changed explicit assumption, objective, or constraint, or a corrected reasoning error. Explain its effect on the recommendation. A user's disagreement alone is not new factual evidence; reconsider the argument without automatically adopting the latest framing.

In a postmortem, distinguish decision quality using information available at the time, reported/observed outcomes, and evidence for causation. Backlash after an apology does not by itself establish that the apology caused it or that another response would have succeeded. Preserve unsupported causal explanations as hypotheses and avoid universal pro- or anti-apology rules.

**Gate:** Each update identifies its basis and remaining unknowns. Recovery claims cite outcomes, not merely agreement among lenses or absence of further criticism.

## Output Pattern

```text
PR CRISIS SNAPSHOT
- Decision, timeline, spread, audience evidence, research constraints

EVIDENCE LEDGER
- Claim, status, source/basis, uncertainty

RESPONSE POSTURE
- Component decisions, evidence, exact responsibility accepted or denied
- Alternatives, selected package, publication gate, change-of-view conditions

STAKEHOLDER PLAN
- Audience segments, intended/adverse readings, all four signal tests, mitigations

RECOVERY ACTIONS
- Approved actions, owners, checkpoints, proof of completion, review triggers

UPDATE BASIS
- New evidence / changed assumptions, objectives, or constraints / corrected reasoning
- Decision-time knowledge, outcomes, causal uncertainty, remaining unknowns
```
